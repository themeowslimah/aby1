Unit 1 · Lesson 1
العَـرْض — الحِوارُ الأَوَّلُ ( أ ) : 👁 اُنْظُرْ وَاسْتَمِعْ وَأَعِدْ

Look, listen and repeat. (Male speakers: Khalid & Khalil)

خالِد : السَّلامُ عَلَيْكُم .

Khālid: As-salāmu ʿalaykum.

Khalid: Peace be upon you.

خَليل : وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلام .

Khalīl: Wa ʿalaykumu as-salām.

Khalil: And upon you peace.

خالِد : اِسْمي خالِد ، ما اسْمُكَ ؟

Khālid: Ismī Khālid, mā ismuka?

Khalid: My name is Khalid, what is your name?

خَليل : اِسْمي خَليل .

Khalīl: Ismī Khalīl.

Khalil: My name is Khalil.

خالِد : كَيْفَ حالُكَ ؟

Khālid: Kayfa ḥāluka?

Khalid: How are you?

خَليل : بِخَيْرٍ ، وَالحَمْدُ لله .
وَكَيْفَ حالُكَ أَنْتَ ؟

Khalīl: Bi-khayrin, wal-ḥamdu lillāh. Wa kayfa ḥāluka anta?

Khalil: Fine, praise be to God. And how are you?

خالِد : بِخَيْرٍ ، وَالحَمْدُ لله .

Khālid: Bi-khayrin, wal-ḥamdu lillāh.

Khalid: Fine, praise be to God.

الحِوارُ الثَّاني ( أ ) : 👁 اُنْظُرْ وَاسْتَمِعْ وَأَعِدْ

Look, listen and repeat. (Muhammad & Sharif)

مُحَمَّد : السَّلامُ عَلَيْكُم .

Muḥammad: As-salāmu ʿalaykum.

Muhammad: Peace be upon you.

شَريف : وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلام .

Sharīf: Wa ʿalaykumu as-salām.

Sharif: And upon you peace.

مُحَمَّد : مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ ؟

Muḥammad: Min ayna anta?

Muhammad: Where are you from?

شَريف : أَنا مِنْ باكِسْتان .

Sharīf: Anā min Bākistān.

Sharif: I am from Pakistan.

مُحَمَّد : هَلْ أَنْتَ باكِسْتانِيٌّ ؟

Muḥammad: Hal anta bākistāniyy?

Muhammad: Are you Pakistani?

شَريف : نَعَمْ ، أَنا باكِسْتانِيٌّ . وَما جِنْسِيَّتُكَ أَنْتَ ؟

Sharīf: Naʿam, anā bākistāniyy. Wa mā jinsiyyatuka anta?

Sharif: Yes, I am Pakistani. And what is your nationality?

مُحَمَّد : أَنا تُرْكِيٌّ . أَنا مِنْ تُرْكِيا .

Muḥammad: Anā turkiyy. Anā min Turkiyā.

Muhammad: I am Turkish. I am from Turkey.

شَريف : أَهْلاً وَسَهْلاً .

Sharīf: Ahlan wa sahlan.

Sharif: Welcome.

Unit 1 · Lesson 9 · Writing — Pen Control

This is a pen-control warmup that must be done on paper — Arabic writes right-to-left and top-to-bottom. Use the textbook grids (pp. 23–28) and the printable practice sheets at the back of the book.

( أ ) نَكْتُبُ مِنَ اليَمينِ إِلى اليَسارِ — Right to Left

Practise tracing horizontal arrows from right to left across the page. This trains the hand to move in the natural direction of Arabic script.

( ب ) نَكْتُبُ مِنْ أَعْلى إِلى أَسْفَلَ — Top to Bottom

Practise tracing vertical strokes from top to bottom. This builds the muscle memory for letter descenders and the basic vertical strokes used in many Arabic letters (e.g. ا، ل، ك).

( ج ) أَعِدْ بِالرَّسْمِ عَلى النِّقاطِ — Trace the Dots

Trace the dotted patterns shown in the textbook, then write freehand. The book provides several rows of these in increasing difficulty.

نَصائِحُ لِلْكِتابَةِ — Tips for the Hand
TipWhy it matters
Hold the pen lightly, not pinched.Arabic calligraphy has natural rhythm — a pinched grip kills the flow and makes tashkīl placement clumsy.
Move your hand from right to left across the page.Letter forms assume this direction — joins, tails and dot placement only make sense when you write the right way.
Practise on lined paper first.Baseline matters — letters sit on the line, with descenders (ج، ح، خ، ع، ل، م، ن، ي) dropping below it.
Tashkīl & dots go on last.Write the skeleton of each word first, then add dots and harakāt — like a teakettle at the end of cooking.
